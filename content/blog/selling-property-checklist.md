Most property disputes and tax notices I see could have been avoided with a single conversation before the token amount changed hands. The law is not especially complicated, but the steps come in a particular order, and missing one early makes everything after it more expensive.

This checklist follows that order. It is written for resident individuals selling or buying land or a building in India, and reflects the position as I understand it on the date of publication. Your own facts matter, so treat this as a map, not as advice for your transaction.

## 1. Before you agree a price: check the title

- **Land records.** For Gujarat, look at the latest 7/12 extract (village form 7/12), form 8A and the mutation entries that explain how ownership changed over time.
- **Chain of documents.** Sale deeds, inheritance or partition documents, and any gift or release deeds, going back at least thirty years where possible.
- **Encumbrances.** Search for mortgages, charges, pending litigation and any government acquisition notices.
- **Permissions.** For non-agricultural use, check the NA order and approved plans; for a flat, the building permission and occupancy certificate.

Title questions belong to your advocate. The point of this step is to raise them before money moves, not after.

## 2. Work out the nature of the gain

For a seller, the first tax question is whether the gain is short-term or long-term. Land and buildings held for more than 24 months are long-term capital assets. Where property was inherited or received as a gift, the period of holding of the previous owner is generally included, and the previous owner's cost is generally taken as the cost.

For long-term gains on transfers made on or after 23 July 2024, the rate is 12.5 per cent without indexation. For land or buildings acquired before that date, resident individuals and HUFs can compare this with 20 per cent after indexation and pay the lower amount. Agricultural land in a rural area, as defined in the law, is not a capital asset at all.

## 3. Compare the price with the stamp duty value

If the stamp duty value of the property is more than the agreed consideration by more than the permitted tolerance (currently 10 per cent), the stamp duty value is generally treated as the sale price for the seller's capital gains. The buyer may also face tax on the difference. Know the stamp duty value before you agree the price, not after.

## 4. Plan the exemptions before the sale, not after

Long-term gains can be reduced or eliminated by reinvesting in a residential house, or in specified bonds within six months of the transfer, within the limits set by the law. The residential house exemption is capped at ₹10 crore of investment, and the bonds route at ₹50 lakh in a financial year.

If the reinvestment will not be complete before the return due date, the unused amount must be deposited in the Capital Gains Account Scheme with a bank by that date. Missing this deadline is one of the most common and most expensive mistakes.

## 5. The buyer's responsibility: TDS

Where the consideration (or stamp duty value, if higher) is ₹50 lakh or more, the resident buyer must deduct tax at 1 per cent from the payment to a resident seller, deposit it with the government using the prescribed challan-cum-statement, and give the seller the TDS certificate. No TAN is needed for this.

Two cautions. If the seller has not furnished a valid PAN, the rate is much higher. And if the seller is a non-resident, an entirely different and higher TDS regime applies, often with a lower deduction certificate obtained in advance.

## 6. On the day of registration

- Payment through banking channels, with each instalment traceable to the agreement.
- Stamp duty and registration fees paid on the correct value.
- Possession letter, original documents and society or utility transfers handed over.
- Both sides keep a complete file: agreement, sale deed, payment proofs, TDS certificate and valuation.

## 7. After the sale

The seller reports the gain in the income-tax return for the year of transfer, claims credit for the TDS shown in their tax statement, and records any exemption claimed with supporting proof. Keep the file for at least eight years.

A property transaction is usually the largest financial decision a family makes in a decade. An hour spent on this list before the token amount is a very good return on time.
