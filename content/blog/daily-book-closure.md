Month-end in most growing businesses looks the same. The first week of the month disappears into reconciliations, missing bills, unexplained bank entries and a scramble to find who approved what. By the time the numbers are ready, they describe a month that is already history.

The fix is not a better month-end. It is a small daily close, done by the same person at the same time, that leaves almost nothing for month-end to do.

## Why daily works

Errors are cheap when they are fresh. On the day a bank entry appears, everyone remembers what it was. Three weeks later it takes a phone call, an email and a guess. A daily close keeps every open item young, and young items are easy to clear.

It also changes behaviour. When the team knows that every voucher will be looked at tomorrow morning, vouchers arrive on time and with their supporting documents.

## The daily checklist

This is the version I use with finance teams. It takes 20 to 40 minutes once the habit is in place.

| # | Check | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | All bank and cash entries for yesterday posted | Books match bank statement balance |
| 2 | Sales invoices raised for every dispatch or service | Dispatch register = invoice register |
| 3 | Purchase bills received and booked | No goods received without a bill entry |
| 4 | Payments approved and released as planned | Payment log matches bank debits |
| 5 | Unexplained entries listed with an owner | Suspense list shared with the owner |
| 6 | Statutory dues due this week flagged | Due list sent to the approver |
| 7 | Supporting documents attached or filed | No voucher without its document |

Every line has an owner and a clear "done when". If a line cannot be completed, it goes on the exceptions list with a name next to it, and that list is reviewed the next morning.

## What changes at month-end

When the daily close is working, month-end becomes a review instead of a rescue: accruals and provisions, inventory, a final look at the exceptions list, and the management report. A close that used to take ten days can realistically come down to three or four.

## What changes at audit

Auditors look for evidence that controls operate consistently. A dated daily checklist, signed off by the person responsible and reviewed weekly, is exactly that evidence. Fewer queries, fewer adjustments and a much calmer audit season are the dividend.

## Making it stick

- **Same person, same time.** Make the close part of someone's job description, not a favour.
- **Visible exceptions.** Share the exceptions list daily; items that sit for three days are escalated.
- **Weekly review.** Ten minutes with the finance head to clear what the team could not.
- **Automate the boring parts.** Bank feeds, invoice numbering and reminders can all be automated once the routine is stable. Automate a habit, never a mess.

The daily close is not glamorous. It is simply the cheapest form of control a business can buy, and it pays back every single month.
